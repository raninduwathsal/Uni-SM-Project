1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & MOTIVATION:
In observational F&B transaction data, consumer purchase patterns are confounded by extraneous variables
such as outlet geographic wealth, day-of-week surges, weather variations, and holiday events. To establish
rigorous causal relationships—rather than simple correlation—between marketing interventions (e.g., dynamic
promotional discounts) and customer purchasing conversion, formal experimental design methodologies must
be evaluated.

2. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGNS EVALUATED:
This consultancy evaluation critically compares two established experimental paradigms:
  A. Completely Randomized Design (CRD)
  B. Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. COMPLETELY RANDOMIZED DESIGN (CRD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Structure:
  Treatments (e.g., 3 promotional discount levels: 0% Control, 10% Loyalty Discount, 20% Flash Deal)
  are assigned completely at random to experimental units (store order transactions or entire store-days)
  without restriction or grouping.
  Mathematical Model: Y_ij = mu + tau_i + epsilon_ij
  where tau_i is the effect of treatment i, and epsilon_ij ~ N(0, sigma^2) is random experimental error.

- Suitability & Situations Where CRD May Be Useful:
  - Online delivery platforms (e.g., Uber Eats / PickMe digital storefronts) where environmental conditions
    are homogeneous and user traffic can be randomly assigned to algorithmic treatments in real time.
  - Controlled laboratory sensory test kitchens where temperature, prep time, and environment are strictly uniform.

- Advantages:
  - Extreme simplicity in randomization and statistical analysis (standard one-way ANOVA).
  - Maximum degrees of freedom for the experimental error term (DF_error = N - k).
  - Robust to unequal sample sizes across treatment arms without loss of orthogonality.

- Critical Limitations in a Physical Restaurant Chain:
  - Vulnerable to severe confounding: If Outlet 40 (high volume) randomly receives more 20% discount treatments
    than Location 85 (low volume), treatment effects are hopelessly confounded with store foot-traffic variations.
  - High residual variance (large MSE): Between-store heterogeneity inflates the error denominator, dramatically
    reducing statistical power to detect subtle promotional effects.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. RANDOMIZED COMPLETE BLOCK DESIGN (RCBD) - RECOMMENDED APPROACH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Structure:
  Experimental units are stratified into homogeneous groups ('blocks') before randomization. Each block contains
  all treatments once.
  Proposed Blocking Factor: 'Store Outlet Location' (or 'Time-of-Day Slots within Outlets').
  Mathematical Model: Y_ij = mu + tau_i + beta_j + epsilon_ij
  where beta_j represents the nuisance variation of block j (outlet base purchasing power).

- Suitability & Situations Where RCBD Should Be Used:
  - Multi-outlet retail chains where store-to-store variance in baseline revenue is substantial (as proven
    in Task 3 where top outlets generate over LKR 800k while smaller outlets generate under LKR 100k).
  - A/B testing promotional elasticity across diverse geographic demographics (commercial business districts
    vs. suburban university branches).

- Expected Advantages:
  - Error Variance Reduction: Removes between-outlet variance (SS_block) from the experimental error sum of squares (SS_error).
  - Increased Statistical Power: Lower MSE results in narrower confidence intervals and higher sensitivity for F-tests.
  - Generalizability: Testing treatments across all blocks demonstrates whether a promotion works universally
    or only in specific store profiles.

- Implementation Challenges & Practical Feasibility:
  - Customer Fairness & Spillage: If two friends visit the same outlet at different times and receive different
    discounts, brand friction may occur. Solution: Block by entire weeks or synchronized digital app voucher releases.
  - Missing Data / Store Disruptions: If an outlet suffers a POS internet outage during one treatment phase,
    orthogonality is broken, requiring more complex unbalanced mixed-model analysis.
  - Practical Feasibility: High. BladeGen Tech's centralized cloud POS system allows scheduled price overrides
    by outlet block seamlessly.

3. SUMMARY RECOMMENDATION:
RCBD with store-outlet blocking is strongly recommended over CRD for future pricing experiments. By isolating
geographic baseline variance, BladeGen Tech can accurately quantify true price elasticity with 30-40% smaller
sample sizes than an unblocked CRD.
