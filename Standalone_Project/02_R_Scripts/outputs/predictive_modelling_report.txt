1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW:
Four distinct statistical classification algorithms were evaluated on 5,893 unseen test item lines
(from a stratified 20% test partition) to predict customer purchasing category (MainCategory:
Beverage, Food, Merchandizing, Deals). Each algorithm was assigned to one group member.

2. GROUP MEMBER ASSIGNMENTS & ARCHITECTURES:
  - Member 1: Multinomial Logistic Regression (Generalized Linear Model / Log-odds)
  - Member 2: Linear Discriminant Analysis / LDA (Bayes' Rule with Pooled Covariance) [CHAMPION]
  - Member 3: Quadratic Discriminant Analysis / QDA (Class-Specific Covariance Matrices)
  - Member 4: Naïve Bayes Classifier (Probabilistic Conditional Independence Estimator)

4. CHAMPION CLASSIFIER SELECTION RATIONALE:
  The selected Champion model is 'Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)' (Member 2).
  - Multi-Metric Equilibrium: LDA leads in Top-1 Accuracy (76.29%), Macro-F1 (76.18%), Balanced Accuracy (73.79%), MCC (0.5996), and Kappa (0.5974).
  - Comparison with QDA: QDA's class-specific covariance estimation introduces singularity risks and parameter variance in minority classes (Merchandising, n=1,588), resulting in lower overall accuracy (73.53% vs 76.29%). LDA pools covariance across classes, providing robust, regularized decision boundaries (Hastie et al., 2009).
  - Comparison with Multinomial Logit: LDA edges out Multinomial Logit in Top-1 Accuracy (76.29% vs 76.18%, delta = +0.11 pp) and Macro-F1 (76.18% vs 75.87%), while maintaining virtual parity in ROC-AUC (91.18% vs 91.20%). Furthermore, LDA yields direct closed-form discriminant score vectors suitable for lightweight POS deployment without iterative optimization.
